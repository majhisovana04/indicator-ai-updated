# Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD)

## Category
Trend/Momentum Indicator

## Definition
MACD is a trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship between two moving averages of a security's price. It helps traders identify potential buy and sell signals.

## Formula
MACD Line = 12-day EMA - 26-day EMA
Signal Line = 9-day EMA of the MACD Line
Histogram = MACD Line - Signal Line

## How to Read It
- When the MACD line crosses above the signal line, it's often read as a bullish signal.
- When the MACD line crosses below the signal line, it's often read as a bearish signal.
- The histogram shows the distance between the two lines — a growing histogram suggests strengthening momentum.

## Common Signals
- Bullish crossover: MACD line crosses above signal line.
- Bearish crossover: MACD line crosses below signal line.
- Zero-line crossover: MACD line crossing above/below zero can indicate a broader trend shift.

## Limitations
- MACD is a lagging indicator, since it's based on moving averages of past prices.
- It can produce false signals in choppy, sideways markets.
- Should be used alongside other indicators for confirmation.

## Example Scenario
If the MACD line crosses above the signal line while both are below zero, some traders read this as an early bullish signal, though confirmation from price action or other indicators is often recommended.
