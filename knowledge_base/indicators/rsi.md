# Relative Strength Index (RSI)

## Category
Momentum Indicator

## Definition

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum indicator that measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes. It helps traders determine whether an asset may be overbought or oversold.

## Formula
RSI = 100 - (100 / (1 + RS))
where RS = Average Gain / Average Loss over a selected period, typically 14 periods.
The result ranges from 0 to 100.

## How to Read It

- RSI above 70 may indicate overbought conditions.
- RSI below 30 may indicate oversold conditions.
- Around 50 usually suggests neutral momentum.

## Common Signals

- RSI crosses above 30 → Possible bullish reversal.
- RSI crosses below 70 → Possible bearish reversal.
- Bullish divergence.
- Bearish divergence.

## Limitations

- RSI can remain overbought during strong uptrends.
- RSI can remain oversold during strong downtrends.
- It should not be used alone for trading decisions.

## Example Scenario

If RSI is 80 while price has been rising quickly, the market may be overbought. This does not guarantee a reversal but suggests traders should look for confirmation from other indicators.
